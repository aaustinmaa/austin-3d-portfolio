# Notes for Claude Code

- Vite + three.js r128 (pinned on purpose; newer three.js changes colour management and the look). Plain JavaScript ES modules, no framework, no TypeScript.
- `src/content.js` holds all visitor-facing text. Keep facts there accurate to Austin's real work; do not invent projects, dates or claims.
- `src/main.js` is still one large module with shared state (`P` player, `camS` camera, `mode`, `T` time). When splitting it, move one system at a time and run `npm run build` plus a manual check in the browser after each move. Self-contained systems already live in their own modules: `oracle.js`, `polyhedra.js`, `constellation.js`, `relics.js`, `hero.js`, `colliders.js`.
- Anything solid needs a collider (`addFrustum` / `addBox` in `src/colliders.js`) so the wanderer can stand on it and arrows stop at it. Visual meshes alone are walk-through.
- In dev, `window.__valley` exposes the player, camera state, relics and `step(n)` (advance the simulation n frames) for checking things from the browser console.
- The visual direction is dark and mysterious: a deep blue-violet night (sky, fog, ground and grass all lean blue-purple), a glowing star, basalt, cyan and gold accents. Avoid bright or fairytale styling, and do not drift toward grey or green. Soft round points of light are part of the mood: a dense twinkling star field, glow just above the meadow grass, motes drifting in the air, and a sparser layer higher up around the stair and beneath the star, so climbing never leaves them behind (all kept clear of the wanderer). They are blue-violet and cyan glows, never geometric crystals or warm lanterns.
- The star and the relics are faceted, polyhedral shapes, not smooth spheres or flat slabs, and they glow softly: hazy auras and halos rather than crisp outlines or wireframes.
- Every polyhedron must be convex: no corner pokes out and none folds in (no stellations, star polygons or compounds). The star's solids are translucent glass (the `glass` material in `src/polyhedra.js`) with a few fine lines, not solid lines and planes. Keep the balance between states: the sleeping star is intricate line work, and the finished star is calmer and ordered (shells aligned), not denser.
- The valley is Austin's own tended place, not someone else's ruin: no broken columns, abandoned structures or crystals stuck in the ground, and no swarms of creatures around the wanderer. Never place anything that can read as a grave marker (a stone, stele or obelisk, least of all one tied to his name). The ground should feel cared for: meadow, pines, natural rock, the lantern-lit path. It is also a forest sanctuary where people come to make wishes: a ring of whole wish pillars (plain eight-sided shafts, square caps you can stand on, a glowing cyan band) stands in the meadow. Pillars stand one by one, never clustered or stacked together. No warm sky lanterns: they clash with the palette.
- Keep the résumé, GitHub and LinkedIn links reachable in one click at all times, and keep every 3D interaction available through a shortcut (keys 1–7, the relic tracker, the Oracle) for visitors who will not play.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (it skips the intro and particle drift).
- Verify with `npm run build` before committing.
- The wanderer is a small doll with no legs, wearing a thin woven robe and a sewn hood with a swept-back pointed tip and short neck drape. Construct the hood as cloth panels running from an independently shaped, forward-facing front edge into a rear seam, following a real fabric hood. Keep the opening small, with a pointed upper arch projecting forward over the forehead and lower corners spreading outward and falling down toward the neck. Follow the supplied hood reference rather than making a large circular opening. Raising the upper arch must not pull it backward over the head. Never construct the hood as a rounded shell with a face hole cut out, and do not add a forehead bulge to force the opening down. Keep the head and neckline raised inside the cloth, preserve the rear tip, and use narrow folded edges and gravity-led drape. The face stays in the hood's shadow except for two small glowing eyes (no modelled face). One pale fabric stands out from the blue-violet surroundings, with a fine woven bump and restrained cloth sheen (`MeshPhysicalMaterial` `sheen`); keep the bow, lantern and night palette. Shape curves use a smooth spline: easing between keys leaves visible terraces. Anchor the face opening and shoulders; give the hem, cuffs and rear hood tip only very subtle cloth deformation with gentle start/stop and turning inertia, respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`. Use CPU vertex deformation in the existing three.js r128 runtime without adding a runtime dependency. No scarves or ribbons.
