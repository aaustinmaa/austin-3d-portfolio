# Notes for Claude Code

- Vite + three.js r128 (pinned on purpose; newer three.js changes colour management and the look). Plain JavaScript ES modules, no framework, no TypeScript.
- `src/content.js` holds all visitor-facing text. Keep facts there accurate to Austin's real work; do not invent projects, dates or claims.
- `src/main.js` is still one large module with shared state (`P` player, `camS` camera, `mode`, `T` time). When splitting it, move one system at a time and run `npm run build` plus a manual check in the browser after each move. Self-contained systems already live in their own modules: `oracle.js`, `polyhedra.js`, `constellation.js`, `relics.js`, `hero.js`, `colliders.js`, `lake.js`.
- Anything solid needs a collider (`addFrustum` / `addBox` in `src/colliders.js`) so the wanderer can stand on it and arrows stop at it. Visual meshes alone are walk-through.
- In dev, `window.__valley` exposes the player, camera state, relics and `step(n)` (advance the simulation n frames) for checking things from the browser console.
- The visual direction is dark and mysterious: a deep blue-violet night (sky, fog, ground and grass all lean blue-purple), a glowing star, basalt, cyan and gold accents. Avoid bright or fairytale styling, and do not drift toward grey or green. Soft round points of light are part of the mood: a dense twinkling star field, glow just above the meadow grass, motes drifting in the air, and a sparser layer higher up around the stair and beneath the star, so climbing never leaves them behind (all kept clear of the wanderer). They are blue-violet and cyan glows, never geometric crystals or warm lanterns.
- The star and the relics are faceted, polyhedral shapes, not smooth spheres or flat slabs, and they glow softly: hazy auras and halos rather than crisp outlines or wireframes.
- Every polyhedron must be convex: no corner pokes out and none folds in (no stellations, star polygons or compounds). The star's solids are translucent glass (the `glass` material in `src/polyhedra.js`) with a few fine lines, not solid lines and planes. Keep the balance between states: the sleeping star is intricate line work, and the finished star is calmer and ordered (shells aligned), not denser.
- The valley is Austin's own tended place, not someone else's ruin: no broken columns, abandoned structures or crystals stuck in the ground, and no swarms of creatures around the wanderer. Never place anything that can read as a grave marker (a stone, stele or obelisk, least of all one tied to his name). The ground should feel cared for: meadow, pines, natural rock, the lantern-lit path. It is also a forest sanctuary where people come to make wishes: a ring of whole wish pillars (plain eight-sided shafts, square caps you can stand on, a glowing cyan band) stands in the meadow. Pillars stand one by one, never clustered or stacked together. No warm sky lanterns: they clash with the palette.
- Keep the résumé, GitHub and LinkedIn links reachable in one click at all times, and keep every 3D interaction available through a shortcut (keys 1–7, the relic tracker, the Oracle) for visitors who will not play.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (it skips the intro and particle drift).
- Verify with `npm run build` before committing.
- Keep the lake's blue-violet reflections sharp enough to read the character and waterfalls. Use long, slow swells that move reflected silhouettes coherently, with no fine ripples or pixel-like shimmer. A faint cyan/violet sheen can drift slowly in broad patches; retain the terrain-depth fade into the shore. Avoid blur taps, coarse mip levels, painted concentric glow rings and random square sparkles. The existing three.js r128 planar reflector matches the renderer's drawing-buffer size and aspect ratio (capped at 2048px per edge and the GPU texture limit), reuses shadows, and refreshes at 24 Hz while the camera is still. Resizing refreshes it immediately. Water waves and colour drift freeze with reduced motion.
- The player's root sits at the ground or platform support height. Keep the lowest posed, deformed robe hem just clear of that surface while grounded, so the doll stands instead of hovering. Apply the same visual offset to the body and bow to preserve the hand grips. Use a fixed model offset in the air so jumps and flights still follow the player's position.
- Join sleeves to the robe with thin, rounded sleeve caps anchored to oval armholes on the torso, not exposed straight tubes or padded shoulder balls. Keep those seams attached while the arm and cuff follow the bow grips. Give arms reaching across the chest a gentle forward bend and keep the drawn bowstring in front of the robe. Sleeve attachments must still follow poses with reduced motion enabled.
- Keep both resting arms close to the body, especially the left bow arm. Bring the held bow and lantern inward with the hands, and keep sleeves slim with restrained shoulder bulges so the arms do not widen the torso's silhouette.
- Keep the character steady when walking or flying: retain the forward body lean, but never add periodic body bobbing, side roll, head sway, arm swings or lantern swings. Subtle deformation belongs only to the loose cloth. The carrying hand must meet the lantern's loop handle, slightly forward of the hip; when that hand draws the bow, the lantern rests on its visible hip hook. Keep the lantern upright as the body leans.
- Full-speed walking uses a .1-radian forward lean (about 5.7 degrees), easing back upright when movement stops. Flight uses .16 radians.
- The wanderer is a small doll with no legs, wearing a thin woven robe and a sewn hood with a swept-back pointed tip and short neck drape. Construct the hood as cloth panels running from an independently shaped, forward-facing front edge into a rear seam, following a real fabric hood. Keep the opening small, with a pointed upper arch projecting only a little forward over the forehead and lower corners spreading outward and falling down toward the neck. Follow the supplied hood reference rather than making a large circular opening. Raising the upper arch must not pull it backward over the head. Never construct the hood as a rounded shell with a face hole cut out, and do not add a forehead bulge to force the opening down. Keep the head and neckline raised inside the cloth, preserve the rear tip, and use narrow folded edges and gravity-led drape. Shape the actual head as a shallow solid with a flat front, rounded temples and a tapered chin, never a sphere. Fit the forehead and temples inside the cloth. The face stays in shadow except for two small glowing eyes. Continue the lower mouth into a short neck drape tucked against the robe, with no wide cape or dangling corners; its top follows the head and its bottom stays anchored to the body when aiming. One pale fabric stands out from the blue-violet surroundings, with a fine woven bump and restrained cloth sheen (`MeshPhysicalMaterial` `sheen`); keep the bow, lantern and night palette. Shape curves use a smooth spline: easing between keys leaves visible terraces. Anchor the face opening and shoulders; give the hem, cuffs and rear hood tip only very subtle cloth deformation with gentle start/stop and turning inertia, respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`. Use CPU vertex deformation in the existing three.js r128 runtime without adding a runtime dependency. No scarves or ribbons.
