# Notes for Claude Code

- Vite + three.js r128 (pinned on purpose; newer three.js changes colour management and the look). Plain JavaScript ES modules, no framework, no TypeScript.
- `src/content.js` holds all visitor-facing text. Keep facts there accurate to Austin's real work; do not invent projects, dates or claims.
- `src/main.js` is still one large module with shared state (`P` player, `camS` camera, `mode`, `T` time). When splitting it, move one system at a time and run `npm run build` plus a manual check in the browser after each move. Self-contained systems already live in their own modules: `oracle.js`, `polyhedra.js`, `constellation.js`, `relics.js`, `hero.js`, `paperbird.js`, `colliders.js`.
- Register every solid render mesh with `addMesh` in `src/colliders.js` (or `inst(..., { solid:'name' })`) so standing, flight, camera obstruction and arrows use its actual triangles. Parent transforms and instanced transforms are included. Moving meshes need `dynamic:true`; CPU-deformed meshes also need `deforming:true`. Exclude light effects, grass, flames, auras and invisible interaction targets. Call `collisionWorld.build()` after static registration and `updateDynamic()` after animation.
- In dev, `window.__valley` exposes the player, camera state, relics and `step(n)` (advance the simulation n frames) for checking things from the browser console.
- The visual direction is dark and mysterious: a deep blue-violet night (sky, fog, ground and grass all lean blue-purple), a glowing star, basalt, cyan and gold accents. Avoid bright or fairytale styling, and do not drift toward grey or green. Soft round points of light are part of the mood: a dense twinkling star field, glow just above the meadow grass, motes drifting in the air, and a sparser layer higher up around the stair and beneath the star, so climbing never leaves them behind (all kept clear of the wanderer). They are blue-violet and cyan glows, never geometric crystals or warm lanterns.
- The Oracle follows selected concept A (left panel of `reference/oracle-concepts.png`): transparent ice-blue armillary ribbons, fine platinum (white-gold) edgework and engravings, a delicate celestial frame, pearl-like joints with star medallions, and a luminous nested polyhedral core. Use `reference/oracle-a.html` during local development to compare its actual 3D form with the concept. Keep the hierarchy between broad translucent ribbons, fine outer filaments and a bright, intricate core. Relics remain softly glowing, faceted polyhedra.
- Every polyhedron must be convex: no corner pokes out and none folds in (no stellations, star polygons or compounds). The Oracle has dedicated procedural glass and filament shaders in `src/oracle-design.js`; changes there should not alter the relic materials. Keep the core as glossy, translucent crystal made of the same nebula glass as the hoops (`nebula` in `src/oracle-design.js`: analytic sky reflections, violet clouds and fine white fractures), with separate back and front surfaces and an inner light that strengthens toward the heart, so the core and the ring share one material. Do not add an opaque inner solid or a frosted/matte surface. Concentrate the metal on the outer armillary as near-white platinum that belongs to its surfaces: a lip and pinstripe on each glass hoop and fine wire on the frame, lit by fixed analytic lights so uneven, leaf-like highlights, soft local bloom and thin four-point star glints appear only where a surface mirrors a light. The core's two outer crystals share that bloom and those glints in ice-white. Never draw glints as round point sprites, and keep the metal from turning yellow, brass or copper. Give the crystal ribbons a substantial curved cross-section and a broad optical face; keep their radii independent of their width and depth. The ribbons are nested hoops, each a zone of one sphere whose broad face curves with the globe, spaced so neither they nor their jewels ever touch while turning. The hoops, metal wire and pearls absorb light (premultiplied alpha), so each draws its far half (beyond the Oracle's centre, seen from the camera) before the core and its near half after, sorted by radius; keep that layering for anything solid-looking added there. The core jewel spans about a third of the globe inside a small ring-and-cross frame; a polar axis and an equatorial diameter end in star-medallion pearls. The core’s two outer crystals carry the same platinum lips as the hoops; keep the inner crystals’ outlines subdued blue-violet, and never let the core read as a wire cage. Its ring profiles are curved glass bands with real thickness. Six chapter jewels restore with the relics, with a bright expanding light ring on arrival; the completed state stays composed and becomes slightly warmer. All decorative rotation, floating and travelling highlights must stop with reduced motion.
- The valley is Austin's own tended place, not someone else's ruin: no broken columns, abandoned structures or crystals stuck in the ground, and no swarms of creatures around the wanderer. Never place anything that can read as a grave marker (a stone, stele or obelisk, least of all one tied to his name). The ground should feel cared for: meadow, pines, natural rock, the lantern-lit path. It is also a forest sanctuary where people come to make wishes: a ring of whole wish pillars (plain eight-sided shafts, square caps you can stand on, a glowing cyan band) stands in the meadow. Pillars stand one by one, never clustered or stacked together. A folded paper crane perches on the last lantern on the right, where the path turns up to the stone stair (`src/paperbird.js`); it stands for Austin's name card. Walking up to it (or clicking it, which walks you there) unfolds it above its lantern into a sheet of writing paper while the dark glass name card opens. It comes undone the way a real crane does, on rigid facets of a true crease pattern: wings up and neck and tail down into the bird base, the petals open into the square base, which opens toward you into a creased square; never swap it for a different sheet or let paper stretch. The paper lights itself evenly per facet instead of taking the scene's lights (the wanderer's lantern would tint it and light it unevenly), and its aura sits behind it, never through it. Leaving a note is a bonus on that paper: clicking it (or N) brings it close to write on, and sending folds it up and it flies off to the star. Notes are posted as JSON to `NOTE.endpoint` in `src/content.js` (empty until a form service is chosen). No warm sky lanterns: they clash with the palette.
- Keep the résumé, GitHub and LinkedIn links reachable in one click at all times, and keep every 3D interaction available through a shortcut (keys 1–7, the relic tracker, the Oracle) for visitors who will not play.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (it skips the intro and particle drift).
- Time everything in real seconds (`dt`, `T`), never in frames: no per-frame counts, steps or decay factors, so motion, durations and effects look the same at any frame rate. Use `Math.exp(-dt*k)` for decay. The main loop already splits slow frames into steps of at most 50 ms.
- Verify with `npm run build` before committing.
- The lake is a flat, still mirror. A planar reflection (`reflectLake` in `src/main.js`, three's Reflector approach with an oblique near plane) redraws the scene from a mirrored camera at three-quarter drawing-buffer size, multisampled on WebGL2, only while the lake is in view, so the pines, pillars, stair and wanderer read crisply upside down. Keep the surface perfectly flat: no waves, ripples, faceted swells or surface stir, no caustic webs, no oil-like iridescent sheen or swirling colour bands, no painted concentric rings or square sparkles. The light column, the star and the nearest lanterns lie on it as faint straight paths of light; through the water the shallows stay clear, deeper water turns blue-violet, and the spring in the middle glows a soft cyan. Keep the terrain-depth fade into the shore.
- The player's root sits at the ground or platform support height. Keep the lowest posed, deformed robe hem just clear of that surface while grounded, so the doll stands instead of hovering. Apply the same visual offset to the body and bow to preserve the hand grips. Use a fixed model offset in the air so jumps and flights still follow the player's position.
- Join sleeves to the robe with thin, rounded sleeve caps anchored to oval armholes on the torso, not exposed straight tubes or padded shoulder balls. Keep those seams attached while the arm and cuff follow the bow grips. Give arms reaching across the chest a gentle forward bend and keep the drawn bowstring in front of the robe. Sleeve attachments must still follow poses with reduced motion enabled.
- Keep both resting arms close to the body, especially the left bow arm. Bring the held bow and lantern inward with the hands, and keep sleeves slim with restrained shoulder bulges so the arms do not widen the torso's silhouette.
- Keep the character steady when walking or flying: retain the forward body lean, but never add periodic body bobbing, side roll, head sway, arm swings or lantern swings. Subtle deformation belongs only to the loose cloth. The carrying hand must meet the lantern's loop handle, slightly forward of the hip; when that hand draws the bow, the lantern rests on its visible hip hook. Keep the lantern upright as the body leans.
- Full-speed walking uses a .1-radian forward lean (about 5.7 degrees), easing back upright when movement stops. Flight uses .16 radians.
- The wanderer is a small doll with no legs, wearing a thin woven robe and a sewn hood with a swept-back pointed tip and short neck drape. Construct the hood as cloth panels running from an independently shaped, forward-facing front edge into a rear seam, following a real fabric hood. Keep the opening small, with a pointed upper arch projecting only a little forward over the forehead and rounded cheek corners curving down toward the neck while preserving the original raised, inward-tucked center of the lower opening. Join the cheek and lower opening curves with continuous tangents, so neither side ends in a sharp folded corner; the neck drape must follow that same rounded edge. Follow the supplied hood reference rather than making a large circular opening. Raising the upper arch must not pull it backward over the head. Never construct the hood as a rounded shell with a face hole cut out, and do not add a forehead bulge to force the opening down. Keep the head and neckline raised inside the cloth, preserve the rear tip, and use narrow folded edges and gravity-led drape. Shape the actual head as a shallow solid with a flat front, rounded temples and a tapered chin, never a sphere. Fit the forehead and temples inside the cloth. The face stays in shadow except for two small glowing eyes. Continue the lower mouth into a short neck drape tucked against the robe, with no wide cape or dangling corners; its top follows the head and its bottom stays anchored to the body when aiming. One pale fabric stands out from the blue-violet surroundings, with a fine woven bump and restrained cloth sheen (`MeshPhysicalMaterial` `sheen`); keep the bow, lantern and night palette. Shape curves use a smooth spline: easing between keys leaves visible terraces. Anchor the face opening and shoulders; give the hem, cuffs and rear hood tip only very subtle cloth deformation with gentle start/stop and turning inertia, respecting `prefers-reduced-motion`. Use CPU vertex deformation in the existing three.js r128 runtime without adding a runtime dependency. No scarves or ribbons.
